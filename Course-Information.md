## 2. Schedule and Class Format

- **Department:** Department of Autonomous Mobility
- **Shared Course:** The course is offered as a shared course, so there are syllabi under two codes, `DAMO425-00` and `DSC-211013`. The `DSC-211013` syllabus lists the course category as major elective (211013), the operating university as Korea University Sejong Campus, the operating department as the Autonomous Driving Systems major, and the English course title as Autonomous Mobile Robot.
- **Type:** Major elective
- **Credits (Hours):** 3 (3) (credits/theory/lab in the `DSC-211013` syllabus: 3/3/0)
- **Intended For:** Fourth-year students
- **Class Time:** Friday period 11
- **Classroom:** Accelerator ICT Convergence Building, Room 324
- **Class Format:** Non-face-to-face and face-to-face classes are combined.
  - Non-face-to-face classes: Theory lectures and presentations.
  - Face-to-face classes: Experiments and lab exercises, with the dates and times arranged with the students at the start of the course.
  - The activity types are lectures, presentations, experiments, and lab exercises. The `DSC-211013` syllabus allocates the class methods as 80% lectures, 10% experiments and lab exercises, and 10% presentations.
- **Attendance:** Attendance is checked (the course is not exempt from attendance checks).
- **Exam Proctoring:** Exams are not held without proctoring.
- **Course Description:** Students learn the structure of robot navigation systems and, in the process of applying it to mobile robots, learn mobility-related hardware and software systems through theory and practice.
- **Link Between the Course Goals, Core Competencies, and Department Goals:** Students learn robot navigation systems and the framework of autonomous driving systems, and the control methods of navigating robot systems suited to indoor and outdoor environments. Based on the theory, they define problems that can arise in real driving environments and build the ability to solve them. The `DSC-211013` syllabus names mobile robots and vehicle systems as the targets of the control methods and adds that the course includes lab exercises using ROS and other tools widely used in real development.
- **Learning Goals:**
  1. Understand the latest technology trends in mobile robots and robot navigation systems.
  2. Build the theory and basic knowledge of robot navigation systems.
  3. Build hardware and software expertise for developing navigating robots.
  4. Foster leadership through system integration.
- **Design Goals:** Define missions in various environments using robot navigation systems and design mobile robot systems that produce efficient results. Find solutions suited to carrying out the missions and propose and design optimized systems based on a theoretical understanding of various mobile robot navigation systems.
- **Core Competencies:** The course is very closely related to foresight, expertise, problem-solving, and creativity, and closely related to innovation.
- **Major Competencies:** Advanced mobility drive system development competency, convergent problem-solving competency
- **Competency-Based Learning Goals in the `DSC-211013` Syllabus:** Learning goals 1-4 correspond, in order, to systems thinking, ICT skill application, convergent problem-solving, and creative innovation.
- **Prerequisites:** The recommended prerequisite and enrollment requirement is programming (Python, C, C++).
- **Course Content Overview:** The course goal and the scope of the whole semester introduced in the week 1 material are in Section 1.1 of the [Week 1 lecture notes](Lecture-Notes/Week01_Mobile-Robot-Trends-and-ROS/Concepts_Lecture.md#11-scope-of-mobile-robot-navigation-systems).
- **Lab Environment:** The Week 3-5 materials explained how to install Ubuntu 18.04 (from a USB drive or in a VirtualBox virtual machine), ROS Melodic, Visual Studio Code, Qt 5.12.12, and Autoware.ai 1.14.0. The installation steps are in the [Week 3](Lecture-Notes/Week03_Autoware-Localization-and-Path-Planning/Concepts_Lecture.md), [Week 4](Lecture-Notes/Week04_Linux-ROS-and-Autoware-Installation/Concepts_Lecture.md), and [Week 5](Lecture-Notes/Week05_ROS-Autoware-Node-Development/Concepts_Lecture.md) lecture notes.

## 4. Course Policies

- **Support for Students with Disabilities:** Students with disabilities can request academic support through the instructor or the Disability Support Center, and on request the instructor works out appropriate measures through a meeting with the student.
- **Academic Integrity:** In every part of completing the curriculum, including attending lectures, writing and submitting assignments, and taking exams, students have the following general duties.
  1. Participate actively in all learning with a sincere and honest mind and a passionate attitude, in line with the purpose and educational philosophy of the university.
  2. Be aware of their position as educated persons receiving higher education, and devote themselves to creative academic inquiry based on moral sensitivity.
  3. Faithfully keep the promises made with faculty or fellow students in connection with educational activities.
  4. Respect the dignity of faculty and fellow students and observe basic courtesy.
- If credits are found to have been obtained through dishonest acts such as plagiarizing assignments, reusing assignments from other courses, or cheating on exams, or through other improper means, the credits are canceled even if already granted, and disciplinary actions such as reprimand, suspension, expulsion, or dismissal may be taken in consideration of the circumstances.
- To raise awareness of academic ethics and encourage creativity, the university runs a plagiarism detection system (SafeAssign in Blackboard), and students must check in advance whether their submission constitutes plagiarism.
